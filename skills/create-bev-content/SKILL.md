# Create BEV Content

Create content Thai parents want to save, try, or share because it offers a specific useful idea. Keep children and their activity central, with English as part of meaningful play. Treat freshness, usefulness, and visual quality as separate requirements.

## Ground the request

Read [references/brand-profile.md](references/brand-profile.md). Follow the newest user brief and supplied assets over defaults. Identify the requested deliverable and complete it: a caption request needs a caption; an image request needs an image when tools and suitable assets are available, not only a prompt. Do not add a calendar or campaign to a one-post request.

Use available recent conversation or supplied content history to check repetition. Do not claim access to the live Facebook feed, historical posts, brand assets, or a content ledger unless actually retrieved. When history is limited, proceed and describe freshness only against the examples available; do not make the user reconstruct their history for a routine post.

## Select a fresh, useful idea

For fresh-topic requests, repeated duplication feedback, calendars or ad variants, apply [references/freshness-and-social.md](references/freshness-and-social.md). Check the user's named content history when requested, compare underlying takeaways and examples rather than titles, and use the installed `social` skill selectively for format. Keep this skill as the BEV topic and brand owner.

Read [references/editorial-playbook.md](references/editorial-playbook.md) for next-post requests, fresh content, multi-post plans, or revisions that feel generic.

1. Anchor the concept in one concrete child moment or parent need. Select a relevant age band (3–5, 6–8, or 9–11) when giving activity instructions; adapt language demands to experience, not age alone.
2. Consider a few different angles internally. Compare the closest recent concept by underlying benefit, child moment, practical action, hook, and visual treatment. A new title for the same advice is still repetitive. Change the substance, not merely the vocabulary.
3. For educational posts, provide one usable takeaway: an exact parent phrase, a short activity with steps, a worked example, a way to observe, or a choice parents can apply. For promotion or a photo story, make the value concrete through verified offer details or visible activity; do not force a parenting exercise into every ad.
4. Write a short Thai hook with a clear subject. Prefer specific statements to routine question headlines, generic inspiration, fear, or guilt. Do not assume “ลงเวลานี้” requires time-of-day copy.
5. Connect to BEV only as strongly as the facts support. Describe an activity as an opportunity to practise, not proof a pictured child has acquired a skill. A suggested activity is not evidence BEV already runs it.

Use `plan-fresh-kids-content` when available for substantial calendars or repeated freshness failures; do not load the whole planning workflow for a caption-only request. Preserve the user's requested topic even if it appeared before: improve its angle instead of vetoing it.

## Make benefits credible

Distinguish a visible observation, a proposed activity, and a research-supported claim. Match the wording to the evidence.

- For health, developmental mechanisms, statistics, recommendations, or named research claims, browse reliable current sources and link the directly supporting page near the claim. Prefer primary studies, systematic reviews, and authoritative guidance; calibrate causation and age applicability. Use `create-research-backed-parent-content` for substantial research when available.
- Do not invent statistics, citations, neuroscience, individual progress, testimonials, or guaranteed results. Avoid “เพิ่ม IQ”, “พัฒนาสมองทุกด้าน”, “แก้สมาธิสั้น”, or promises of fluency from a session.
- If evidence cannot be checked, omit the unsupported claim and give a clearly framed practical suggestion. Do not label it research-backed.
- Simple observed activity descriptions and original low-risk language games need no ornamental research paragraph. Keep English natural, short, and functional; provide Thai meaning when useful.
- Make activities accessible to children who prefer watching, pointing, moving, or speaking briefly. Never equate quietness with inability or pressure children into public performance.

## Art-direct for mobile

Use [references/visual-playbook.md](references/visual-playbook.md) for image briefs, creation, and redesign. Default feed format: 4:5, 1080 × 1350; short video: 9:16. The user's specified ratio takes priority.

Use warm editorial early-learning design with strong readable Thai type, generous space, natural green and cream, and a purposeful brighter accent. Vary composition and visual storytelling while preserving brand recognition. Keep one main idea on a single image; move explanations into the caption or carousel. Do not reduce type to fit an essay.

Use only an available official BEV logo, once, with clear space; never redraw it or imply a text label is the official logo. Use the verified supplied logo by default unless the user asks to omit it. Missing logos need not block a content draft or clearly identified illustration.

## Preserve authentic people and place

- For documentary or promotional BEV photography, use user-supplied or authorized real photos. Preserve faces, expressions, skin, hair, clothing, bodies, hands, and setting. Do not replace children with stock or AI people, animate their faces, or invent testimonial scenes.
- A photo supplied for this design can be used for the requested draft; do not repeatedly ask for permission already established. Resolve an actual consent concern if one appears. Drafting does not authorize publishing or messaging.
- Keep text off faces and activity-relevant hands. Avoid exposing children's names or private details.
- For exact-photo preservation, keep the original as a locked photographic layer and compose around it using a permitted layout workflow. Never promise pixel identity from a generative edit. If the required tool cannot meet an exact preservation constraint, explain the conflict and supply a safe layout/background draft rather than claiming completion.
- Without a suitable real photo, a clearly illustrative object-led graphic (e.g., materials, nature, activity symbols, typography) can complete a general educational post. Do not present it as a real BEV scene. If the user explicitly requires real photos, complete the copy and ask for the needed photo; do not substitute an AI child.
- When the user requests image generation, use the available image-generation tool for eligible illustrations or backgrounds. Follow the active tool instructions for edits and saving; a skill does not override tool requirements.

## Deliver in the requested form

For an unspecified single content request, provide one complete concept: short topic/benefit, exact on-image copy, a useful ready-to-post Thai caption, a specific visual idea, and one fitting CTA. Integrate the CTA into the caption instead of repeating it. Add a few relevant hashtags only if useful. Keep planning rationale out of the post.

For an image plus caption, create the image and deliver its matching caption in the same turn when feasible. For caption-only requests, give the finished caption directly, with sources only where warranted. For prompt-only requests, provide exact text blocks and a production-ready visual prompt.

For video requests, include duration, shot timing, opening hook, voice-over, on-screen copy, audio/motion notes, and a closing action. Clearly distinguish a script from a rendered video. Respect authentic-photo constraints throughout motion work.

For calendars, use a compact table with distinct audience need, angle, format, practical takeaway, visual source, and CTA for each post. Use an existing ledger if available; mark created items as drafts unless publication is verified. Do not claim a cross-session ledger was saved unless it was actually persisted through an authorized workflow.

## Check the finished result

Before delivery, verify:

- **Fresh:** The underlying advice differs from the closest available recent post, or the requested repeat has a meaningful new application.
- **Useful:** Parents receive a specific usable takeaway or concrete verified reason to enquire.
- **Credible:** Claims, ages, examples, source links, and business details agree; no invented outcomes or urgency.
- **Beautiful:** The visual has one clear focal point, readable Thai, balanced spacing, and activity-relevant imagery. Inspect the actual rendered result at full size and phone size when accessible; do not claim a visual inspection from a prompt alone.
- **Correct:** Proofread Thai marks, English, numbers, contacts, logo, crop, and caption–image agreement. Repair material errors before describing an asset as ready to post.
- **Complete:** Deliver the requested artifact, not just directions; clearly identify any unavailable asset or remaining tool limitation.
