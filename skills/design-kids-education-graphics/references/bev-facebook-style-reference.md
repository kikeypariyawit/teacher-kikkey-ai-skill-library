# BEV Facebook style reference

This reference captures the visual grammar the user approved on 2026-09-15. It is a style system, not a template to copy literally. Use the current brief, real BEV assets, latest verified facts, and current platform requirements over anything here.

## Reference DNA

The approved reference family combines **real child activity, outdoor nature, bold Thai editorial type, warm illustrated framing, and compact information modules**. It should feel like a professionally art-directed children’s education campaign rather than a generic school poster or a stock template.

Core characteristics:

- child or family moment is the emotional anchor
- Thai headline is large, friendly, and immediately legible on a phone
- natural green / cream base with brighter yellow, orange, blue, or pink accents
- leaves, wood, hand-drawn marks, or nature motifs are used as framing devices, not decoration everywhere
- supporting photos can appear as scrapbook/editorial cards when each image adds a different story beat
- benefit icons are simple and limited; they clarify, not decorate
- composition may be lively, but the reading path stays obvious
- warmth and playfulness come from authentic expressions, sunlight, nature, and activity—not from excessive stickers

## Four approved composition families

### A. Adventure hero + supporting collage

Use for enrollment, activity showcase, outdoor adventure, or event invitation.

- one dominant authentic child/action photo
- large Thai headline across a clean or painted text field
- 2–4 small supporting activity photos only when they show different experiences
- optional short benefit row or age/session block
- organic framing such as leaves or wood can reinforce the outdoor identity
- CTA is visually strong but secondary to the message

Avoid filling every edge with slogans, badges, arrows, or duplicate claims.

### B. Storybook offer card

Use for price, package, event, or comparison posts.

- illustrated or object-led storybook environment can frame the offer
- price/package cards must be the clearest information layer
- large numbers, short labels, strong comparison hierarchy
- keep promotional facts exact and verified
- decorative family/adventure illustration must never compete with the price and session information

Use this family only when the post is truly about an offer. Do not turn educational content into a sales card.

### C. Real-photo skills collage

Use for “what children practise here,” activity benefits, or trust-building.

- authentic activity photos remain the proof layer
- central or upper headline explains one main promise
- 3–5 benefits maximum, each with a simple icon and short phrase
- supporting handwritten accents can add warmth, but essential facts stay in strong sans serif type
- every photo should contribute a distinct action, emotion, or skill context

Do not claim a child acquired a skill just because the photo shows participation. Phrase outcomes as opportunities to practise unless evidence is available.

### D. Quiet parent editorial

Use for parenting, family connection, emotional reflection, or save/share posts.

- one warm lifestyle photo or authentic family moment
- large text column with generous breathing room
- one highlighted thought or phrase
- 2–4 short practical takeaways with simple icons
- softer contrast and calmer composition than adventure posts
- caption can carry nuance; image should remain emotionally readable at a glance

This family should feel grown-up and trustworthy, not childish.

## BEV Facebook feed rules

Default feed canvas remains **4:5 (1080 × 1350)** unless the user or placement requires another ratio. Treat this as a BEV production default, not a claim about a permanent Meta specification.

For a single Facebook feed image:

1. The first read should work in roughly two seconds.
2. One dominant message owns the image.
3. Headline should usually be 1–3 short lines.
4. Keep body copy off the artwork unless it is essential to the decision.
5. Use large Thai type, strong foreground/background separation, and a clear primary subject.
6. Keep the background visually controlled around text.
7. One or two icon styles are enough; avoid mixed clip-art families.
8. Check the actual image at phone width before delivery.
9. Keep faces, hands, and safety equipment unobstructed.
10. Use the official BEV logo once, unchanged, with clear space.

## Research-backed platform notes

Platform guidance is volatile. Re-check current Meta documentation when placement, ads delivery, or exact platform behavior matters.

Research reviewed 2026-09-15:

- Meta emphasizes creative quality, fast attention hooks, visible contrast, testing multiple creatives, and previewing ads on device before launch.
- Meta’s own AI thumbnail guidance emphasizes large readable type, a clear primary subject, simple icons, and uncluttered backgrounds because feed visuals appear small.
- Facebook’s design guidance favors clarity, creating space for people and stories, and consistent brand systems that can flex across content.

These findings support the BEV rules above, but they do **not** justify promising reach, virality, or conversion.

## Research gate for educational posts

Before putting a developmental or educational benefit on the image, classify it:

- **Visible observation:** what the child is literally doing in the supplied photo or activity description.
- **Practice opportunity:** a reasonable skill the activity gives children a chance to practise.
- **Research-supported claim:** a broader developmental, health, language-learning, or behavioral statement that needs evidence.

Use `bev-education-researcher` for the third category. Prefer primary research, systematic reviews, major institutions, or professional associations. Preserve age and context limits. Do not turn correlation into causation.

For Facebook packaging, translate research into **one safe parent-facing takeaway**. Put nuance and sources in the caption when useful; do not crowd the image with citations or an academic paragraph.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- generic AI children when real BEV photography is required or available
- generated pseudo-logos
- too many wooden signs, leaves, clouds, stickers, speech bubbles, and badges in one frame
- five different font personalities
- tiny Thai body text used to force an essay into one image
- decorative English slogans that compete with the Thai message
- fake testimonials or invented child outcomes
- guaranteed claims such as “เห็นผลแน่นอน,” “เพิ่ม IQ,” or “พูดคล่องใน 1 วัน”
- copying the supplied references literally; extract their hierarchy, warmth, activity energy, and editorial framing instead

## Final visual test

A BEV Facebook graphic should pass these questions:

- Can a parent understand the main idea without zooming?
- Is the child/family moment or offer immediately clear?
- Does every decorative element have a job?
- Is the design recognizably BEV without repeating the same layout every time?
- Are all educational claims calibrated to evidence?
- Are Thai spelling, logo, prices, dates, contact details, and crops correct?
- Would the post still look polished if all nonessential stickers were removed?
