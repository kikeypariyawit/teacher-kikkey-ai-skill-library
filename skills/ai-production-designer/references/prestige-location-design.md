# Prestige Location Design

## Design for story, not decoration

Every major environment should answer:
- Who built/controls this place?
- What behavior does it encourage?
- What behavior does it punish?
- What is maintained perfectly?
- What is neglected?
- Where can power be seen spatially?

## Make recurring locations memorable

Use three anchor categories:
1. **Structural anchor** — staircase, balcony, atrium void, long corridor, arch, stage.
2. **Material/light anchor** — warm timber ribs, concrete skylight, green glass, tungsten sconces.
3. **Story anchor** — trophy wall, student art installation, family portrait, nurse station, security gate.

Repeated anchors create continuity more reliably than repeating a paragraph of adjectives.

## Large hall / campus example

For a premium creative-arts faculty atrium:
- three-story open void
- bridges/balconies on upper levels
- suspended student installation
- large daylight source from clerestory/skylight
- registration/activity islands rather than one empty lobby
- stairs as vertical movement anchor
- controlled banners/posters tied to the event
- students grouped by activity rather than random crowd noise

This makes scale visible in 9:16 through height and depth.

## Mansion example

Avoid "luxury = gold everywhere."
Use:
- controlled materials
- proportion
- art selection
- staff/service movement
- acoustic quiet
- long sight lines
- restricted doors/rooms
- personal objects that reveal family history

## Hospital example

Show function:
- nurse station
- triage/room numbering
- transport routes
- carts/monitors only where operationally plausible
- shift-change density
- directional practical lighting and sound perspective

## Continuity

Track:
- location master
- scene anchor
- crowd density
- furniture/prop changes
- light/time/weather
- temporary event dressing

Do not let every shot invent a new building.
