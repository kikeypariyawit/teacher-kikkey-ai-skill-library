# Prestige drama: production design, performance and camera

Interpret “100-million production” as an aspirational screen standard, not a verified budget, guaranteed output, or instruction to spend money. Achieve scale through coherent geography, authored environments, controlled acting, motivated light, selective spectacle and an edit that makes the world feel inhabited.

## Series look bible
Record the chosen aspect ratio, emotional point of view, contrast and color progression, texture, weather logic, recurring compositions, lens family and camera restraint. For vertical delivery, stage depth and height; reserve room for subtitles and platform UI after checking the intended output. Do not fake cinematic quality with black bars or attach “8K / masterpiece” to every prompt. Lens terms describe a visual target unless actual capture metadata exists.

For each location, specify:
- location ID, architecture, era, materials, wear, practical light sources and acoustic character;
- floor-plan anchors, entrances/exits, windows, camera side, character routes and three repeatable landmarks;
- foreground/midground/background layers, populated versus private zones, hero prop and meaningful negative space;
- day/night and weather variants, and which spatial facts cannot change.

Make wealth, institutional power or poverty legible through access, service choreography, maintenance, materials and people's behavior. Avoid turning every location into an interchangeable marble lobby. A hospital at shift change needs different operational details and sound from a private auction.

## Scope the spectacle
Choose a small number of emotionally necessary signature sequences; do not force a spectacular shot into every short episode. For each sequence write:
1. Dramatic reason: what choice, reveal or loss requires the scale?
2. Reveal hierarchy: what is seen or heard first, then withheld, then disclosed?
3. Geography and human anchor: where is the protagonist within this scale?
4. Coverage: establishing view, playable medium/close performance, decisive insert and consequence.
5. Practical AI construction: separately generated environment, limited foreground action, reactions and sound bridges.
6. Reduced-complexity alternative preserving the same story turn.

Use crowds primarily as controlled background groups or short establishing shots. Avoid demanding several speaking crowd members, elaborate hand contact, an identity transformation and a complex camera move in one generation. Hide construction with motivated cuts; do not label a static storyboard as finished VFX.

## Performance direction
For each beat name the character's objective and playable tactic: disarm, conceal, test, protect, delay, threaten or surrender. Then specify an observable behavior and its change. Replace “sad and cinematic” with a concrete action such as holding eye contact through an accusation, swallowing the reply, then quietly moving a key out of reach.

Specify listening and reaction time. Let a close-up carry a choice without constant cutting. Distinguish public composure from private emotional leakage. Preserve the approved facial identity through makeup, posture and lighting changes.

For romance, direct mutual attention, distance, hesitation and reciprocation among adults; no automatic smiling. Choose restrained or heightened performance according to the actual brief. Do not escalate a requested romantic scene into explicit sexual detail.

## Camera and lighting contract
Each camera move needs a dramatic trigger and endpoint. Use a static frame for withheld power, a controlled push for realization, a lateral reveal for hidden spatial information, or another motivated choice. These are options, not fixed formulas.

State shot size, camera height, viewpoint, approximate perspective, focus priority, movement path and landing composition. Avoid contradictory directions such as simultaneous locked-off and orbiting camera. Preserve the action axis, eyelines and screen direction; establish a new axis visibly when needed.

State key-light source/direction, fill or negative fill, practicals, background separation, exposure priority and atmosphere. Keep skin and eyes readable where drama depends on them. Use motivated color separation; do not apply teal/orange, haze, rain or shallow focus everywhere. Keep weather, shadow direction and practical fixtures consistent across coverage.

## Edit and sound design
Build an edit map from actual dramatic changes. Include performance holds, reaction shots, inserts, match-on-action and sound bridges only where useful. Distinguish generated clip duration from the segment used in the edit; overlapping transitions must not inflate runtime.

Plan dialogue, room tone, ambience, foley, spot effects, score entry/exit and intentional silence. Mark diegetic versus score. Sound can imply a larger world beyond the frame; do not let music flatten every reveal. Use licensed or original audio assets and record their provenance when obtaining them.

Specify recurring musical or sonic motifs by dramatic association, not an unsupported promise to reproduce a copyrighted score. Include subtitle language, readable timing and an export target appropriate to the user's platform. Verify changing technical/platform requirements when relevant.
