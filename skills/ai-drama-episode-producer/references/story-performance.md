# Story and cast development for cinematic series

## Story engine
Begin with the audience promise, protagonist's active want, competing need, opposition, season question and cost of failure. Build a season escalation ladder and a setup/payoff ledger. Do not equate virality with random twists or guarantee reach.

Each episode must have its own dramatic question, decision chain and partial payoff. A hook creates a specific question through understandable action; a cliffhanger follows a consequential change. Adapt timing to genre and runtime rather than inserting reversals mechanically.

For each scene record objective, obstacle, tactic, turning point, changed power/knowledge/emotion, consequence and visual opportunity. Test “because / therefore”: does the next event follow an action or established pressure? Give antagonists goals and constraints. Preserve smart characters' ability to examine evidence; do not sustain conflict only by everyone refusing to speak.

Plant meaningful evidence before a reveal and track who could observe it. If a protagonist recognizes someone, specify the cue, when they saw it and what they choose to conceal. Teach through consequences and choices rather than a detached moral speech. Keep comedy from erasing grief or danger without an intended transition.

## Cast bible
Separate immutable identity from changing appearance and state:
- character ID, adult age where relevant, story role and approved reference asset IDs;
- stable facial structure, distinctive features, hair baseline, build and silhouette;
- goals, wound, contradiction, fear, moral boundary, secret and stress response;
- speech rhythm, register, social mask, behavior with each key relationship;
- approved wardrobe/look IDs, accessories and transformation logic;
- relationship and knowledge state by episode, plus evidence supporting changes.

Avoid indistinguishable “beautiful man / beautiful woman” casting. Differentiate silhouette, posture, gesture, voice and social behavior. Follow the project's casting brief without turning one project's ethnicity, gender or beauty preferences into a rule for every series.

A confidence makeover changes styling, posture and choices while preserving identity. Exact face continuity requires available reference assets; memory or prose alone cannot prove a face matches. Where references are inaccessible, finish reference-independent work and label matching as unverified rather than inventing approval.

## Dialogue and timing
Use the user's chosen spoken language. Thai production notes and English generation prompts can accompany either Thai or English dialogue. Preserve exact dialogue strings and label speakers; do not translate spoken lines silently.

Write intention against subtext and character-specific vocabulary. Avoid characters narrating information visible on screen. Use silence, interruption, breath and physical action when they change a beat. Distinguish on-screen speech, voiceover and inner thought.

Time each line with a language-appropriate read-through estimate and include pauses, reactions and action. Thai text is not reliably timed by whitespace word count. If no audio was actually read or synthesized, mark timing as estimated. Reduce or split overloaded lines; do not fit a long monologue into a short generated clip by assigning an arbitrary duration.

Deliver a finished script plus scene-state handoff, not just a premise, when a full episode is requested.
