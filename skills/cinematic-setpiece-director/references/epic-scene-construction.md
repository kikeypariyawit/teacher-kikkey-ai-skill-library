# Epic Scene Construction for AI Drama

## Core principle

A large scene should be designed as a sequence of controlled reveals, not as one overloaded prompt.

### Seven-layer construction
1. **World reveal** — establish scale and the rules of the space.
2. **Human anchor** — make one character emotionally legible.
3. **Pressure field** — show what the environment/crowd makes difficult.
4. **Approach** — movement through geography with a clear objective.
5. **Decisive beat** — reveal, choice, confrontation or rescue.
6. **Public consequence** — crowd/event/location responds.
7. **Aftermath** — quiet image or state change that proves the cost.

Not every sequence needs all seven, but the order is useful for diagnosing flat spectacle.

## Hero image design

A hero image should answer three questions at once:
- Where are we?
- What does the character feel/want?
- Why is this moment different now?

Prefer one memorable image over five generic wides.

## Complexity triage

When a shot is unstable, remove complexity in this order:
1. redundant camera movement
2. unnecessary crowd specificity
3. simultaneous secondary actions
4. exact end-frame lock
5. complex contact
6. extra named identities

Protect the principal face, story action and geography last.

## Scale through edit

Scale can be built from:
- one strong wide
- medium protagonist coverage
- one event insert
- one reaction group
- offscreen sound
- a return to the changed wide

This often feels larger than a single inconsistent crowd shot.

## Action scenes

For fire, smoke, escape, storm or physical danger:
- establish safe geography first
- isolate one action objective per shot
- show cause before effect when continuity matters
- separate VFX-heavy environment from identity-critical acting when needed
- preserve injury, dirt, wetness, smoke direction and prop state across cuts

## Quiet scenes in large spaces

Do not assume spectacle requires motion.
A still character framed against a vast active environment can create more scale than an aggressive camera move.

## Vertical 9:16

Exploit:
- high ceilings
- staircases
- balconies
- banners
- shafts of light
- crowds receding vertically
- foreground shoulders/objects creating depth

Keep faces large enough to read on a phone.
