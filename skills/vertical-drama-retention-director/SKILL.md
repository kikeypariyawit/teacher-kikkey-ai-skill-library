# vertical-drama-retention-director

## Purpose

Turn a sound episode structure into a high-retention vertical-drama viewing experience. Own attention architecture, hook clarity, curiosity gaps, emotional pulse, reveal spacing, visual reset opportunities and cliffhanger pressure.

This skill does not replace story causality. `ai-drama-story-engine` decides what happens and why; this skill decides how the approved dramatic information is paced for a vertical audience.

## Use when

Use for:
- 9:16 short drama, micro-series and social episodic fiction
- weak openings or slow first 15 seconds
- episodes that feel flat despite a workable story
- retention maps and reveal timing
- adapting longer scenes into vertical pacing
- strengthening next-episode desire

Do not use to force fake twists, arbitrary danger or clickbait that breaks the story.

## Required inputs

Use the best available combination of:
- approved story beats / script or episode outline
- audience promise and genre
- target runtime and platform
- episode question and cliffhanger intent
- reveal inventory / facts that must not be spoiled early
- current character and relationship state
- user pacing preferences

If story causality is not yet stable, hand back to `ai-drama-story-engine` before optimizing attention.

## Core principle

At every meaningful beat ask:

**What question, desire, fear or expectation is carrying the viewer into the next beat?**

Retention comes from unresolved dramatic energy, not from constant noise.

## Workflow

1. **Define the audience promise**
   - state the emotional experience viewers came for: revenge, romance, dread, mystery, wish-fulfillment, comedy, tenderness, status reversal, etc.
   - protect that promise across the episode

2. **Audit the opening 1–3 seconds**
   Prefer one strong immediately legible trigger:
   - contradiction
   - accusation
   - dangerous action already in progress
   - emotionally loaded discovery
   - impossible-looking situation
   - high-status reversal
   - intimate interruption
   - visual evidence that creates a question

   Avoid generic setup narration, scenic throat-clearing or context the viewer does not yet need.

3. **Build the attention chain**
   For each major beat record:
   - current viewer question
   - answer/reveal delivered
   - new question created
   - emotional direction
   - visual change opportunity

   A beat should usually answer something while opening something else.

4. **Design retention shifts**
   Use shifts such as:
   - information reveal
   - status reversal
   - changed intention
   - emotional exposure
   - new obstacle
   - physical movement into a new spatial relationship
   - unexpected reaction
   - evidence insert
   - silence after impact
   - attraction spike
   - threat escalation

   Do not enforce a mechanical timer. As a heuristic for short social drama, inspect whether there is a meaningful change roughly every 5–15 seconds when the scene energy supports it.

5. **Use visual resets deliberately**
   Suggest a new composition, insert, reaction, location zone, movement, foreground reveal or scale change only when it renews attention and serves the scene.
   Do not cut merely because several seconds passed.

6. **Protect emotional contrast**
   Alternate pressure with controlled pauses, hesitation, intimacy, disbelief, humor or silence so strong beats have contrast.

7. **Compress exposition**
   Replace explanation with visible evidence, conflict, object use, reaction, behavior or delayed disclosure whenever possible.

8. **Strengthen the final 10–20%**
   The ending should create a concrete next-question through:
   - revelation
   - irreversible decision
   - betrayal
   - arrival
   - danger
   - evidence
   - relationship reversal
   - identity/status reveal
   - impossible choice

9. **Run anti-clickbait QA**
   The hook and cliffhanger must be paid for by the episode. Do not promise a different story than the viewer receives.

## Output contract

For a full episode return:
- audience promise
- opening-hook diagnosis and final hook
- beat-by-beat retention map
- viewer-question chain
- recommended reveal order
- visual reset opportunities
- emotional contrast notes
- weak-retention repairs
- cliffhanger pressure statement
- handoff notes to `cinematic-director`, `performance-director` and shot planning

## Retention map schema

For each beat:
- Beat ID
- Approximate edit position or relative order
- Viewer question
- Dramatic event
- Answer delivered
- New question created
- Emotional direction
- Visual reset: yes/no + reason
- Risk: confusion / repetition / dead air / over-explanation / false promise

## Final QA

- The first seconds are understandable without backstory dumping.
- Each major beat changes the viewer's expectation, knowledge or emotion.
- Curiosity is specific, not vague.
- Visual resets serve dramatic meaning.
- Quiet beats still contain unresolved pressure.
- Repetition is removed.
- The ending earns a next-episode question.
- No claim of guaranteed virality or guaranteed retention is made.

## Operating rules

- Story causality remains owned by `ai-drama-story-engine`.
- Do not introduce random twists merely to create activity.
- Platform heuristics are guidelines, not laws.
- Preserve explicit user runtime, tone and pacing preferences.
- For a prestige cinematic brief, favor legible high-impact beats over hyperactive editing.
- Keep outputs directly usable by direction, performance and editing passes.
