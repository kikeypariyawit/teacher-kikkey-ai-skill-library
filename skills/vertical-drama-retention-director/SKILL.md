# vertical-drama-retention-director

## Purpose

Turn a sound episode structure into a high-retention vertical-drama viewing experience. Own attention architecture, hook clarity, viewer-question continuity, emotional pulse, reveal spacing, visual reset opportunities, completion pressure and cliffhanger continuation.

This skill does not replace story causality. 'ai-drama-story-engine' decides what happens and why; this skill decides how approved dramatic information is paced and surfaced for a vertical audience.

## Use when

Use for:
- 9:16 short drama, micro-series and social episodic fiction
- weak openings or slow first 15 seconds
- episodes that start strong then sag
- retention maps and reveal timing
- adapting longer scenes into vertical pacing
- improving completion and next-episode desire
- diagnosing actual retention-graph drops
- preventing repetitive hook/cliffhanger patterns

Do not use to force fake twists, arbitrary danger or clickbait that breaks the story.

## Research evidence rule

Initial seconds matter on short-form platforms, but timing heuristics are not laws. Verify current platform guidance before making current claims.

## Required inputs

Use the best available combination of:
- approved story beats / script or episode outline
- audience promise and genre
- target runtime and platform
- episode job, question and cliffhanger intent
- promise/debt ledger
- reveal inventory / facts that must not be spoiled early
- current character and relationship state
- user pacing preferences
- retention timestamps / completion data when available

If story causality is not stable, hand back to 'ai-drama-story-engine' before optimizing attention.

## Core principle

At every meaningful beat ask:

**What specific question, desire, fear, expectation or emotional debt carries the viewer into the next beat?**

Retention comes from unresolved dramatic energy plus meaningful payment—not constant noise.

## Retention architecture

Track four layers:

1. **Entry** — does the opening immediately make a legible promise?
2. **Carry** — does each beat give the viewer a reason to stay through the middle?
3. **Payment** — does the episode answer or transform enough prior questions to feel rewarding?
4. **Continuation** — does the ending create a specific new pressure worth another episode?

A hook without payment is a trust leak.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the audience promise

State the emotional experience viewers came for:
- revenge
- romance
- dread
- mystery
- wish-fulfillment
- comedy
- tenderness
- status reversal
- survival
- social humiliation/recovery
- another explicit genre promise

Protect that promise across the episode.

### 2. Audit the opening seconds

Prefer one immediately legible trigger:
- contradiction
- accusation
- dangerous action already in progress
- emotionally loaded discovery
- impossible-looking situation
- high-status reversal
- intimate interruption
- visual evidence that creates a question

The opening should:
- begin near meaningful pressure
- make sense without a lore lecture
- deliver on packaging/previous cliffhanger
- establish whose emotion matters
- create a specific question

Avoid:
- logo/preamble
- scenic throat-clearing
- generic narration
- context the viewer does not need yet
- false shock unrelated to the episode

### 3. Build the viewer-question chain

For each major beat record:
- current viewer question
- answer/reveal delivered
- new question created
- emotional direction
- whether a prior story debt is paid
- visual change opportunity

A beat should often answer something while opening something else.

Do not carry five equally important questions at once.

### 4. Protect the middle

The middle is not filler between hook and cliffhanger.

Inspect:
- whether the protagonist changes tactic
- whether power shifts
- whether new information alters interpretation
- whether relationship distance changes
- whether the viewer receives a partial payment
- whether the same emotion repeats too long

If the middle only “continues arguing”, redesign the dramatic movement before adding cuts.

### 5. Design retention shifts

Use shifts such as:
- information reveal
- status reversal
- changed intention
- emotional exposure
- new obstacle
- physical movement into a new spatial relationship
- unexpected reaction
- evidence insert
- silence after impact
- attraction spike
- threat escalation
- tactic change
- payoff of an earlier setup

Do not enforce a mechanical timer.

As a heuristic for short social drama, inspect whether long stretches pass without a meaningful change in expectation, knowledge, emotion or spatial relationship.

### 6. Use visual resets deliberately

Suggest a new:
- composition
- insert
- reaction
- location zone
- movement
- foreground reveal
- scale change

only when it renews attention and serves the scene.

Do not cut merely because seconds passed.

### 7. Create emotional contrast

Alternate pressure with controlled:
- hesitation
- intimacy
- disbelief
- humor
- silence
- false relief
- vulnerability

Quiet beats must still contain unresolved pressure or meaning.

### 8. Compress exposition

Replace explanation with:
- visible evidence
- conflict
- object use
- reaction
- behavior
- delayed disclosure
- a character making the wrong inference

If exposition is required, attach it to a decision or threat.

### 9. Manage payment cadence

For active promises/questions label:
- opened
- partially answered
- paid
- transformed into a stronger question

Every short episode should usually provide some meaningful payment.

Do not use cliffhangers as an excuse to withhold all satisfaction.

### 10. Strengthen the final section

The ending should create a concrete next-question through:
- revelation
- irreversible decision
- betrayal
- arrival
- danger
- evidence
- relationship reversal
- identity/status reveal
- impossible choice

The strongest cliffhanger changes the story state.

Avoid ending only because the runtime is over.

### 11. Rotate cliffhanger pressure

Track recent endings.

If several episodes end with the same shape, rotate among:
- reveal
- reversal
- decision
- arrival
- betrayal
- danger
- evidence
- emotional confession/interruption
- moral dilemma

Rotation is a diagnostic, not a quota.

### 12. Analytics-based repair

When retention data is available:
1. map drop/spike timestamps to beat IDs
2. inspect what the viewer was asked to understand or feel there
3. compare adjacent episodes and traffic sources when possible
4. distinguish confusion, repetition, dead air, false promise, weak acting/editing and story weakness
5. propose 1–3 competing causes
6. repair the smallest likely cause
7. re-measure after release

Do not assume every retention drop is solved by faster cutting.

### 13. Run anti-clickbait QA

The hook, packaging and cliffhanger must be paid for by the story.

Do not promise:
- a character/event that barely appears
- a reveal the episode refuses to address
- a genre the episode does not deliver
- a danger that resets without consequence

## Output contract

For a full episode return:
- audience promise
- opening-hook diagnosis and final hook
- beat-by-beat retention map
- viewer-question chain
- promise/payment map
- recommended reveal order
- middle-retention repairs
- visual reset opportunities
- emotional contrast notes
- cliffhanger pressure statement
- recent-cliffhanger repetition check
- analytics diagnosis when data is supplied
- handoff notes to 'cinematic-director', 'performance-director' and shot planning

## Retention map schema

For each beat:
- Beat ID
- Approximate edit position or relative order
- Viewer question
- Dramatic event
- Answer delivered
- New question created
- Promise/debt status
- Emotional direction
- Visual reset: yes/no + reason
- Risk: confusion / repetition / dead air / over-explanation / false promise / unpaid setup

## Final QA

- The opening is understandable without backstory dumping.
- It delivers on packaging or the previous episode's ending.
- Each major beat changes expectation, knowledge, power or emotion.
- The middle contains a real dramatic movement.
- Curiosity is specific, not vague.
- At least one meaningful promise is paid or transformed.
- Visual resets serve dramatic meaning.
- Quiet beats retain pressure.
- Repetition is removed.
- The ending changes next-state pressure.
- Analytics are interpreted cautiously.
- No claim of guaranteed virality or guaranteed retention is made.

## Operating rules

- Story causality remains owned by 'ai-drama-story-engine'.
- Do not introduce random twists merely to create activity.
- Platform heuristics are guidelines, not laws.
- Preserve explicit user runtime, tone and pacing preferences.
- For prestige cinematic briefs, favor legible high-impact beats over hyperactive editing.
- Keep outputs directly usable by direction, performance and editing passes.
