# Cinematic production design and direction

## Translate the ambition
Treat “production 100 ล้าน” as a creative quality target, not an actual budget, technical setting, or promised equivalence to a studio film. Translate it into an emotional audience promise, coherent visual grammar, dimensional spaces, believable performances, selective spectacle, and controlled sound/editing. Respect requested scale; do not automatically shrink an epic brief to two actors in one room. Propose achievable coverage and layered production where a single complex generation is unreliable.

For a new high-ambition production, briefly consider three substantially different directions through world, dramatic conflict, palette, scale, and production risk. Choose the strongest and execute. For an approved series, develop within its established direction; do not reopen its premise every episode.

## Story scale and set pieces
Connect private stakes (trust, shame, family, desire) to public consequences (a ceremony disrupted, an institution challenged, a crowd changing allegiance). Size alone does not create stakes. Build a set piece as objective → geography → obstacle → tactic → reversal → costly choice → aftermath. Each stage changes what the protagonist can do. Let a quiet aftermath register the cost.

Use spectacle selectively: identify the episode's hero image and decisive performance beat; allocate detailed visual work there. Use a wide geography shot, controlled protagonist coverage, practical-looking inserts, and coherent crowd reactions to imply scale. Never silently replace a specifically requested crowd or location with an empty room. A wide shot and sound must agree about occupancy.

## World and art department
For each recurring location define L-ID, narrative role, time/era, architecture, material palette, spatial anchors, entrances/exits, practical light sources, weather, recurring dressing, and usable camera sides. Give objects an owner and a reason for wear. Distinguish wealth through materials, space, service behavior, and controlled detail rather than universally gold interiors.

Design foreground/midground/background with one attention priority. Give background people distinct tasks and reaction timing; avoid synchronized cheering, duplicated faces, frozen extras, or busy movement behind a critical line. Stage a few readable zones; describe large anonymous crowds broadly and use separate coverage for named characters. Maintain crowd density and reaction state across cuts.

For VFX-heavy shots, separate subject, environment, foreground occlusion, effects, and sound in the plan. Specify contact, shadow, reflection, perspective, interaction, and state changes that must match. A compositing plan is only a plan until compatible editing tools execute it. Typography essential to the plot should be an exact edit overlay or separately controlled insert.

## Cinematography bible
Define aspect ratio/composition, point of view, contrast, skin rendering, color progression, lens-feel vocabulary, camera distance, depth of field, movement rules, and motivated light. Camera/lens labels in prompts express visual intent, not verified physical capture or guaranteed model parameters.

Choose proximity and perspective from the beat: wider spatial views reveal power and geography; tighter views reveal a decision or concealed reaction. Allow deep focus when the environment contains evidence. Do not prescribe shallow depth of field, orange/teal, rain, haze, anamorphic flare, or camera orbit in every scene.

Motivate light from windows, fixtures, stage sources, street light, or established world rules. Track direction and practical color across coverage; retain facial detail appropriate to the scene. Create depth through separation and selective contrast, not simply making the frame dark. Let palette change with the character arc when useful.

Block actors before choosing camera movement. Define screen positions, eyelines, action axis, and destination. Crossing the axis should be an intentional reorientation or a visible move. Move the camera when the viewer gains information or emotional proximity; a held shot can be the most cinematic choice. Use movement holds and reaction time so the decisive beat is readable.

## Acting with scale and specificity
Build a performance beat from objective, obstacle, tactic, trigger, visible reaction, attempted recovery, and new tactic. Use playable verbs: reassure, corner, conceal, provoke, protect, bargain, resist. Include listening and reaction before the next line.

For “ละครเวที / เล่นใหญ่ / อินเนอร์จัด” honor heightened performance. Choose clear physical commitment, breath, vocal attack, precise pauses, and meaningful emotional changes. Calibrate amplitude to framing: a wide shot can carry a larger gesture; a close-up can carry the same intensity through eyes, jaw, interrupted breath, and controlled stillness. Do not automatically turn intense acting into restrained naturalism, or make every line a scream.

Mark baseline → trigger → peak → residue across the scene. Avoid instantaneous crying, smiling during grief, all characters at maximum intensity, or expression frozen by identical first/last frames. Give each actor a distinct stress response and relationship-specific distance/touch behavior. Preserve age, face, voice and wardrobe anchors while allowing full pose and expression changes.

## Editorial and sound design
Plan coverage for decisions, listening, evidence, entrances, and exits, not only alternating speaking close-ups. Cut on a meaningful glance, action, discovery, or power shift. Use sound bridges and J/L cuts where they carry continuity or anticipation. Keep pauses that alter interpretation. Distinguish source-clip duration, handles, final edit duration, and transition overlap.

Build a cue sheet with dialogue, room tone, environmental perspective, Foley, event SFX, music motif, and intentional silence. Track voice identity, register, space/reverb, breath, and emotional residue. Avoid music masking the clue or dialogue. Let sound suggest offscreen scale consistently with the setting; do not put every dramatic reveal under the same boom.

For a rendered film inspect voice/line correctness, intelligibility, sync, noise, peaks, and transitions using available media tools. Use delivery specifications only when verified for the destination. Do not label audio checked after reading a prompt or inspecting a still.
