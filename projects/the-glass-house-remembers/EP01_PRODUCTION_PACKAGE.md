# EP01 — THE PORTRAIT

Status: DRAFT FOR FINAL CREATIVE APPROVAL
Series: THE GLASS HOUSE REMEMBERS
Format: 9:16 vertical cinematic drama
Target final runtime: ~92 seconds
Dialogue: English
Genre tone: gothic mystery romance / prestige psychological thriller
Lead characters: Mara Vale, Adrian Blackthorn, Mrs. Rowan

## Episode promise
Mara accepts a private restoration commission and discovers the damaged portrait she was hired to restore depicts a woman with her exact face—painted in 1975.

## Main viewer question
Why does a portrait painted twenty-four years before Mara was born have her face?

## Secondary viewer question
Did Adrian know what Mara looked like before he hired her?

## Opening hook
Macro restoration action immediately reveals an impossible facial match before the audience receives context.

## Cliffhanger
Mrs. Rowan sees Mara standing beside the restored face and instinctively calls her “Miss Evelyn.”

---

# FINAL EPISODE SCRIPT

## SCENE 1 — RESTORATION STUDIO — NIGHT
Rain taps against tall windows. The room is warm tungsten light surrounded by darkness.

A cotton swab rolls slowly across smoke-dark varnish.

Beneath the soot, a painted hazel-green eye appears.

MARA freezes.

She looks from the eye in the painting to her own reflection in the black glass of a cabinet.

MARA, under her breath:
“That’s my eye.”

She clears another narrow strip. A cheekbone. The curve of a mouth.

The resemblance is no longer arguable.

A voice behind her:

ADRIAN:
“You should stop for tonight.”

Mara does not turn.

MARA:
“Who is she?”

Adrian’s silence lasts too long.

Mara finally faces him.

ADRIAN:
“Evelyn Blackthorn.”

MARA:
“Your family?”

ADRIAN:
“My great-aunt.”

Mara looks back at the portrait.

MARA:
“She looks like me.”

ADRIAN:
“Yes.”

Mara studies him. Not surprised enough.

MARA:
“You knew.”

ADRIAN:
“I knew what was under the smoke.”

MARA:
“And you hired me anyway?”

ADRIAN:
“I hired the conservator who was recommended to me.”

MARA:
“That isn’t an answer.”

He steps closer but stops outside her personal space.

ADRIAN:
“No. It isn’t.”

Mara notices a tarnished brass plaque on the table, removed from the frame.

She lifts it.

INSERT — engraved letters:
EVELYN BLACKTHORN
1948–1975

Mara’s breathing changes.

MARA:
“When was this painted?”

ADRIAN:
“Summer of 1975.”

MARA:
“I was born in 1999.”

A quiet sound at the open door.

Mrs. Rowan stands there holding fresh white camellias.

She sees Mara beside the exposed face in the portrait.

The flowers slip slightly in her hands.

MRS. ROWAN:
“Miss Evelyn…”

Silence.

Mara turns toward her.

Mrs. Rowan goes pale.

MRS. ROWAN, correcting herself:
“Miss Vale.”

Mara looks between Rowan, Adrian and the portrait.

CUT TO BLACK.

# RETENTION MAP

0–4 sec — visual contradiction: Mara uncovers her own eye in an old portrait.
4–14 sec — resemblance expands from coincidence to impossible match.
14–28 sec — Adrian enters; his controlled reaction creates suspicion.
28–47 sec — the portrait identity is named: Evelyn Blackthorn.
47–63 sec — Mara directly accuses Adrian of knowing.
63–77 sec — date reveal establishes the impossible timeline.
77–92 sec — Mrs. Rowan calls Mara “Miss Evelyn,” converting resemblance into recognition.

Do not add unrelated jump scares. The tension comes from recognition and withheld knowledge.

# PERFORMANCE MAP

## Mara
Start: professional concentration.
Trigger 1: painted eye resembles hers.
Tactic: verify visually before reacting emotionally.
Trigger 2: Adrian is not surprised enough.
Tactic: interrogate him with precise questions.
Trigger 3: 1975 date.
Shift: control gives way to bodily fear—shallower breath, stillness, voice softer rather than louder.
Final trigger: Rowan says “Miss Evelyn.”
End state: fear becomes certainty that the house contains human knowledge of the resemblance.

## Adrian
Objective: keep Mara from learning too much too quickly without lying outright.
Tactic: minimal truthful answers.
Attraction/recognition should not be played romantically yet; it is contained shock plus guilt.
When Mara says “You knew,” hold eye contact. Do not look away like a simple liar.

## Mrs. Rowan
The “Miss Evelyn” mistake must feel involuntary and emotionally old, not theatrical horror.
One small intake of breath after seeing the portrait and Mara together is enough.
